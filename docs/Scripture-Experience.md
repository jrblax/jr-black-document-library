# Treasury of Truth

## The Scripture Experience

### Purpose

The Scripture Experience defines how readers encounter, explore, and study the Bible through the Treasury of Truth.

The Treasury of Truth shall remain Scripture-centered. Sermons, lessons, articles, annotations, subjects, and related resources exist to serve the biblical text rather than compete with it.

The reader should always be able to begin with Scripture and move naturally into related resources, subjects, passages, and explanatory material.

## Guiding Principle

The Treasury of Truth is not merely designed to help readers find documents.

It is designed to help readers follow the Scriptures.

Every major pathway through the library should encourage continued engagement with the biblical text.

## Primary Entry Points

Readers should be able to begin through:

- A Bible book
- A chapter
- A verse or passage
- A canonical subject
- A resource title
- An author
- A series
- A collection
- Full-text search

Scripture shall remain the preferred and most prominent entry point.

## Bible Browsing

The library should provide a clear division between:

- Old Testament
- New Testament

Readers should be able to select a Bible book and then browse its chapters.

Each Bible book page may eventually include:

- Book overview
- Chapter navigation
- Resources based on the book
- Major subjects
- Biblical people
- Biblical places
- Biblical events
- Related series and collections

## Chapter Experience

A chapter page should help readers understand what the Treasury of Truth contains concerning that chapter.

It may include:

- Resources using the chapter as a primary passage
- Resources citing the chapter as a supporting passage
- Canonical subjects associated with the chapter
- Related passages
- Biblical people, places, and events
- Approved annotations
- Hidden Jewels, when editorially justified

The chapter itself must remain the organizing center of the page.

## Passage Experience

A passage page should identify:

- The biblical reference
- Resources that expound the passage
- Resources that cite or apply the passage
- Primary and related subjects
- Parallel or related passages
- Relevant people, places, and events
- Approved annotations

The library should distinguish between a resource that expounds a passage and one that merely references it.

## Resource Relationships to Scripture

Resources may relate to Scripture through defined relationships such as:

- Primary text
- Supporting text
- Quotation
- Allusion
- Exposition
- Application
- Comparison
- Contrast
- Prophecy
- Fulfillment

These relationships should be explicit rather than inferred solely from free text.

## Annotations and Hidden Jewels

Annotations may clarify linguistic, historical, cultural, geographical, literary, archaeological, or textual matters.

Hidden Jewels shall be used only when they materially illuminate the inspired text.

Hebrew and Greek insights shall be included only when the original text contributes a meaningful distinction, emphasis, or range of meaning not fully apparent in the cited Bible translation.

Annotations shall never be used to force upon Scripture a meaning foreign to its language, context, or apostolic witness.

## Related Scripture

The Scripture Experience should help readers continue through the biblical text.

Related passages may include:

- Parallel passages
- Prophetic connections
- Fulfillment passages
- Doctrinally related passages
- Narrative continuations
- Explicit biblical cross-references

Related passages must be selected transparently and responsibly.

## Search and Discovery

Scripture search should support:

- Bible book
- Chapter
- Single verse
- Verse range
- Full reference
- Partial reference
- Resources connected to a passage
- Subjects associated with a passage

Search results should distinguish between primary and supporting Scripture relationships.

## Reader Experience

The Scripture Experience should be:

- Clear
- Reverent
- Uncluttered
- Searchable
- Accessible
- Consistent
- Useful to ministers, teachers, students, missionaries, and general readers

The design should encourage study rather than distraction.

## Guiding Question

Every Scripture-centered page should answer:

> Where may the reader go next in the Word of God?

## Final Principle

The Treasury of Truth shall organize all supporting knowledge around Scripture while preserving the authority, clarity, and centrality of the biblical text.
